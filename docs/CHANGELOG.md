### C-01 — First-person instead of over-the-shoulder (2026-08-29)

- **Section changed:** §4.2 (Walk and explore), §6 AC-Movement, §11 (NPC life).
- **Was:** Camera was over-the-shoulder (camera 4m behind the player, FOV 42, looking at the player's chest). Player avatar always visible.
- **Now:** First-person. Camera is the player's eyes. No avatar visible during play. Player avatar IS visible in cutscenes (intro, end-of-day "leave the office" cinematic) where the camera dollies around them.
- **Reason:** The user found the over-the-shoulder controls confusing ("the mouse rotates the camera around the character, I want to simulate that I'm moving the direction of the character"). First-person = the player IS the trainer. Wall collision is trivial. Matches standard 3D-RPG convention.
- **Implementation:** `src/engine/controls.ts` is rewritten to use `camera.position = player.position + (0, EYE_HEIGHT, 0)` and `camera.rotation = (pitch, yaw, 0)`. Mouse delta updates yaw/pitch directly. The mouse does NOT orbit around the player; the player avatar turns to face the yaw direction.

### C-02 — Mouse-look mode and navigation decision (2026-08-29) — REVISED 2026-08-29 (ADR-0007: Pattern D)

- **Section changed:** §4.2 (Controls spec).
- **Was:** Mouse always rotates the view. Player's mouse "got out of the screen" because the mouse was driving the view continuously.
- **Now (Pattern D, per ADR-0007):** Default state is **free mouse** (OS cursor visible, mouse does not rotate the view). RMB-hold = mouse-look mode (cursor hidden, mouse moves rotate the view). Space (or Tab-when-not-roster) toggles mouse-look for trackpad / MacBook users. Roster panel is the primary way to choose who to talk to from a distance. LMB in free-mouse mode is a screen-space raycast: if it hits an NPC, the player walks to face the NPC and the dialogue opens. LMB in mouse-look mode is a center-screen raycast (FPS-style crosshair). Tab = roster toggle (existing).
- **Reason:** The user explicitly asked: "Mouse should be blocked after click on the game area maybe? To let me move without getting out of the screen? OR, well... we have these buttons, so we need mouse also to click these buttons to choose the characters in the office... we need to decide how we design navigations." And later (verbatim): "we should have the mouse all the time available and need to hold the mouse button to rotate so we always can use mouse to click objects." After research (`.agent-briefs/mouse-look-report.md`), the best-practice for a 3D RPG with economy/simulation is **Pattern D** (Deus Ex / Skyrim / WoW model): free mouse for UI, RMB for view rotation, walk-to-face for in-world interactions, plus a key toggle for trackpads.
- **Rejected alternatives (see ADR-0007 §3.4 for full reasoning):**
  - **Always-rotating mouse with pointer lock (Pattern A)**: too aggressive, breaks the UI (player has to press Esc every time to click a button). Verdict in research: 4/10 suitability.
  - **Always-free mouse, view rotates with arrow keys only**: feels sluggish, doesn't match the "look around" expectation.
  - **Cursor-on-edge pan (Pattern C)**: incompatible with first-person interior navigation. Verdict: rejected.
  - **Free mouse + edge pan + RMB snap (Pattern E)**: unpredictable, causes rotational nausea in first-person. Verdict: rejected.
  - **Plain Pattern B (no Space/Tab toggle)**: works on desktops with a real RMB, fails on trackpads. Pattern D adds the toggle.
- **Implementation:** `src/engine/controls.ts` is rewritten. State machine: FREE_MOUSE (default) / MOUSE_LOOK_HOLD (RMB down) / MOUSE_LOOK_TOGGLE (Space). LMB mousedown calls `raycastNpc(x, y)` in free-mouse mode and triggers `walkToFace(npc)` + `openDialogue(npc)`. WASD works in all states. `contextmenu` is `preventDefault`-ed. `src/engine/interaction-raycaster.ts` is a new pure module for the raycast math. `src/engine/walk-to-face.ts` is a new pure function for the walk planner. `src/ui/cursor.ts` is the custom pixel-art cursor. Detail in ADR-0007 §4.
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/controls.test.ts` extended with FPS camera state, pitch clamp, mouse-look toggle. `tests/unit/interaction-raycaster.test.ts` (new). `tests/unit/walk-to-face.test.ts` (new). TDD per PR-8.

### C-04 — Camera must NEVER go through walls; first-person solves this (2026-08-29)

- **Section changed:** §11.4 (Intro), §6 AC-Movement.
- **Was:** Over-the-shoulder camera could go through walls (the user reported: "camera can get out from the office right now, and we can't see through the walls so very often I don't see the character when we are in above the sholder view").
- **Now:** First-person. The camera is the player's eyes. The player cannot go through walls (AABB collision), so the camera cannot either. The wall-clipped-camera bug is impossible by construction.
- **Reason:** The user proposed two solutions: (a) first-person, (b) camera collision. We chose (a) because it is simpler and matches the standard 3D-RPG convention.
- **Implementation:** None needed beyond C-01. The AABB collision in `src/engine/collision.ts` already prevents the player from going through walls; the camera is the player's eyes, so it follows.

### C-05 — Cutscenes show the avatar in 3rd person (2026-08-29)

- **Section changed:** §11.4 (Intro).
- **Was:** The player is in 1st person the whole time, even during cutscenes. The player never sees what they look like.
- **Now:** During the intro and end-of-day cinematics, the camera is in 3rd person (over-the-shoulder or free orbital) so the player can see their own avatar. The camera animates from 3rd person to 1st person at the end of the intro (a 0.5s tween).
- **Reason:** The user: "Even with first person view, in cutscenes and intros we should be able to see ourself, the main character, from 3rd person perspective, so we know how we look. And then we animate the camera move to change to the first person perspective."
- **Implementation:** During the intro cinematic, the camera is detached from the player. After the cinematic ends, the camera tweens (lerp position, slerp rotation) from the cinematic-end position to the 1st-person player-eye position over 0.5s. The `controls.ts` module exposes a `setMode(mode: 'fps' | 'cinematic' | 'free')` API for the cinematic to call.

### C-09 — Walk-to-face: player and NPC face each other before dialogue (2026-08-29)

- **Section changed:** §4.3.
- **Was:** Player could open a dialogue while standing anywhere — including behind the NPC. The conversation opened with the player looking at the NPC's back.
- **Now:** When the player initiates a conversation (E, click, roster card), the player avatar auto-walks to a "stand in front of the NPC" position (1.5m in front of the NPC's facing direction). The NPC rotates to face the player. Dialogue opens when both are in position and facing each other.
- **Reason:** "When we talk to somebody we should simulate that we walk to this person and this person should move also in our direction, like it would look on us when we talk. now I talk to the back..."
- **Implementation:** A new `src/engine/walk-to-face.ts` module. Pure function `planWalkToFace(player, npc): {target, npcTargetYaw}` returns a target position and the NPC's target yaw. The dialogue system calls `walkToFace` before opening. If the player cancels (Esc), the walk aborts.

### C-10 — Multi-turn dialogues (4-8 turns minimum per conversation) (2026-08-29) — REVISED 2026-08-29 (Lucas: "no hard number, just RPG-style")

- **Section changed:** §4.3.
- **Was:** Dialogue was single Q-and-A. Pick an option, dialogue ends. "Only one question and one answer? thats it? Is it how it looks like in any real office?"
- **Was (initial 4-8 turn target):** Each NPC conversation is 4-8 player turns minimum.
- **Now (revised per Lucas 2026-08-29):** **No hard turn count.** Lucas: "I don't have one number in my head as a goal. I just need this game to be real game, not a demo, so we need enough options, branching, decisions trees etc to make this a real simulation, with simulation of relations, previous actions influencing future actions and dialogues and answer options. Like in real RPG!"
- **What this means in practice (from the opencode dialogue-count report):**
  - **5 layers per NPC:** greeting pool + topic threads + follow-up branches + memory callbacks + gated options.
  - **Per Tier-A NPC (Bartek, Zosia, Maciek):** 220-300 authored lines, 50+ nodes, 5-6 threads.
  - **Per Tier-B NPC:** 130-150 lines, 3-4 threads.
  - **Per Tier-C NPC (Pawel, Janusz, Burek):** 50-110 lines, 1-2 threads.
  - **Total:** ~2,300 authored strings across ~730 tree nodes (13x today's volume, ~100x perceived variety).
  - **Hard cap on a single conversation: 14 turns** (the dialogue walker enforces it; deeper arcs are split across multiple conversations, not single ultra-long ones).
  - **RPG-style branching:** previous decisions change future options, gate new threads, shift NPC memory. The dialogue tree is not a single path; it is a graph of options that depend on `state.relationships[npcId]`, `state.flags`, `state.day`, `state.lastTopic[npcId]`.
  - **Memory callbacks:** NPCs reference past conversations, e.g. "you said yesterday you hated standups" — this is what makes the simulation feel real.
- **Reason:** "Not only simulation of the in-work life, but also meetings with clients, daily standups, courses where we are a trainer and we are in a class and people are listening (or not... ;) we can have funny situations with challenges" AND "Like in real RPG" with branching and decision influence.
- **Implementation:** `src/ui/dialogue.ts` is rewritten to support multi-turn trees, gated options, NPC reactions per option, and conversation memory. The dialogue data model in `src/content/dialogues.ts` is upgraded to support 50+ nodes per important NPC with conditional branches. The 5-layer structure (greetings/threads/branches/callbacks/gated) is the canonical pattern.

Glass walls (or one big glass wall facing the main office) with a **three.js glass/transparency effect** so the player can see through to the main office.
  - Optional: a **small lobby / reception** at the building entrance where the player spawns. Not required, can be skipped for scope.

Add this to PRD, ADR, Plans to do not forget!!"
- **CRITICAL constraint:** The existing main office MUST NOT BE BROKEN. The user: "DO NOT BREAK existing room! I like how it looks like!" This means:
  - The 20x20 office stays in the same world coordinates, same NPCs, same desks, same floor, same walls.
  - The new rooms are added by REMOVING one or two walls of the existing office (the east or north wall, depending on layout) and adding new rooms on the other side.
  - The current `OBSTACLES`, `OFFICE_BOUNDS`, NPC positions, and player spawn stay valid.
  - If a collision wall is removed to create a doorway, the doorway width is at least 2.5 units (two NPCs can walk through side by side).

  - A `THREE.Mesh` with `THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial` (or `MeshStandardMaterial` with `transparent: true, opacity: 0.25`).
  - Refraction / reflection for the "glass" look: `transmission: 0.9, thickness: 0.5, roughness: 0.1, ior: 1.5` (requires `MeshPhysicalMaterial`).

Marek's morning is "at his desk in the main office"; his mid-morning is "at the coffee machine in the kitchen." His walk goes through the doorway. ### C-39 — NPC rotates ANIMATED to face the player on dialogue, returns to previous direction after (2026-08-31)

- **Section changed:** `src/engine/npc-controller.ts`, `src/ui/dialogue.ts`, `src/engine/walk-to-face.ts`.
- **Was:** when the player initiates a conversation, the NPC's `rotation.y` is set to the face-the-player angle INSTANTLY. - **Now:**
  - **On dialogue open:** the controller stores the NPC's current `rotation.y` as `previousFaceY` and sets a target `facePlayerY`. ### C-48 — NPC-vs-NPC collision model: hard separation, right-hand avoidance, blocked-stop (2026-09-01)

- **Source (Lucas, 2026-09-01):** "We have issue with object colision for NPC, they try to get in the same place, sometimes just in opposite directions and they just can't and they block, jumping as crazy! high frequency strange jumps. ... I prefer to don't make them ghost and don't allow going through other NPC, they should just change direction a little bit and go around in opposite direction (e.g. in this case both turn right). If they colide and block, they should at least stay and don't jump! Than also should never come so close to eachother, they come like touching eachother ... so we need minimal distance, small but needed. If rotating to go around doesn't work they should change direction imho. when they colide thay can keep talking like they do now, to simulate a meeting, but now they can block and talk for whole morning until afternoon lunch moves them to the kitchen... They should not jump again! They now jump and talk so it is impossible to read... jumping text..."
- **Was:** the only NPC-vs-NPC mechanism was `computeAvoidancePush` - a soft 0.3 m post-move nudge, applied only when BOTH NPCs were moving. Four compounding defects: (1) no hard collision, so NPCs ghosted through each other and through stationary people; (2) the push side was chosen by `sign(dot(perpendicular, away))`, which is ~0 for a head-on encounter and flips on float noise every frame - each NPC re-picked a side 60x/s, so blocked pairs vibrated laterally at frame rate (the "crazy jumping", and the position-anchored bubble text jumped with them); (3) the walk-cycle phase advanced by TIME (`speed*dt`), not by actual displacement, so a blocked NPC marched in place at full bob; (4) there was no deadlock policy at all - a blocked pair vibrated until the next period transition re-planned it.
- **Now (Lucas's decisions, implemented as the standard 3-layer local-movement model: prevent -> cure -> deadlock policy):**
  1. **Prevention - deterministic avoidance steering** (`computeAvoidanceSteer`, replaces `computeAvoidancePush`): when another NPC (moving OR standing - walkers now route around people at the coffee machine) is within `AVOID_RADIUS = 1.2 m` and inside a ~120-degree forward cone, the walker steers laterally AWAY from the neighbor's side; on a head-on bearing both NPCs dodge to their OWN right (deterministic right-hand rule, so the pair agrees without communication - Lucas: "both turn right"). Strength scales with proximity and dt (frame-rate independent). The `priorityFor` ranking is gone - right-hand symmetry needs no priority.
  2. **Cure - hard separation** (controller, every frame, all visible pairs): minimum center distance `MIN_SEPARATION = 0.7 m` (2 x 0.3 m radius + 0.1 m buffer - Lucas: "minimal distance, small but needed"). Overlapping pairs are pushed apart along the center axis; two walkers split the correction 50/50, walkers always yield to settled/dwelling NPCs (settled people are immovable, so desks stay put and the schedule is never fought). Each displacement is clamped against furniture AABBs per axis (existing `isSpawnBlocked` pattern). Ghosting is impossible by construction.
  3. **Deadlock policy - blocked means STOP, then change direction:** if a walking NPC's actual displacement is under `BLOCKED_PROGRESS_RATIO = 0.25` of `walkSpeed * dt`, it is blocked: the gait amplitude eases to 0 within ~0.25 s (stands still, no bob, no leg swing - Lucas: "if they colide and block, they should at least stay and don't jump!"). If blocked within 1.2 m of the walk target, it finishes the walk and settles at the nearest free ring position around the target (side by side = the "meeting"; Lucas: "they can keep talking like they do now, to simulate a meeting" - chatter pairs at <= 2.5 m already cover the talking). Otherwise after `BLOCKED_REPATH_AFTER_S = 3` it re-plans the path from its current position (ONE re-path attempt per block episode, Lucas: "if rotating to go around doesn't work they should change direction"); if that doesn't help it waits - and chats - until the obstacle moves or the period changes. Any real progress resets the block clock.
  4. **Gait keyed to actual progress:** `updateWalkCycle(state, dt, speed, progressMetres)` advances the phase by the metres ACTUALLY moved this frame, and `WalkCycleState.amplitude` eases toward 1 while moving and 0 while blocked (~6/s). Marching-in-place is impossible by construction, so bubble text can never "jump" from a vibrating NPC again.
- **Rejected alternatives:** ghost-through (Lucas explicitly forbade: "I prefer to don't make them ghost"); RVO/ORCA velocity-obstacle solver (correct but heavy - needs neighbor velocity negotiation and a library for good results; overkill for ~15 NPCs on a waypoint graph); priority-based one-sided yielding (the old model - it is exactly what produced the deadlock, because both NPCs' priorities made them insist or both yield into each other).
- **Known limits:** settled-settled pairs are not separated (the scheduler owns those positions - desks must never drift); the player is not yet an obstacle for NPC steering (NPCs can still walk through the player - follow-up if it bothers Lucas).
- **Acceptance criteria:** (1) Two NPCs on crossing or identical paths never come closer than 0.7 m and never overlap. (2) A head-on pair turns right and passes each other - no vibration, no deadlock. (3) A blocked NPC stands still (root y pinned to baseY, no leg/arm swing, no bob) while the block lasts. (4) A blocked NPC near its target settles beside the blocker and they chat; a blocked NPC far from its target re-paths once, then waits. (5) A walker passing a standing NPC routes around them instead of ghosting through. (6) The morning door crowd (everyone spawning on the same point) spreads apart instead of stacked.
- **Amended 2026-09-01 (v1 playtest, Lucas: "it is better, no more jumping when they block, and they keep small distance, but now they are.... dancing in the ring, rotating :D ... maybe the way they try to avoid eachother makes them dancing. They should not rotate around theirself, they should rotate at place, move forward and rotate again. or totally change direction after a while if still blocked" and "They can talk for a while, but from a distance, and then they should use different method to get around eachother"):** v1's PREVENT layer was continuous per-frame lateral steering, which curved every walk path into an arc - two (or three) NPCs arcing around each other read as a slow ring dance, and near-target arcs could orbit forever. v2 replaces continuous steering with DISCRETE, straight-line discipline:
  1. **Stop at a distance** (`walkBlockedAhead` capsule check): anyone standing inside the straight walk line (1.1 m lookahead, 0.5 m half-width) freezes the NPC where they are - they stop ~1 m short, face each other, and the chatter system makes it a meeting ("talk for a while, but from a distance"). No pressing into each other.
  2. **Turn in place, walk straight** (`detourWaypoint`): after 1.2 s blocked the controller splices ONE detour waypoint into the path, 0.9 m to the walker's right and 0.6 m ahead (after 2.6 s: to the left). Movement is always straight segments between path points; rotation happens in place between segments - orbiting is impossible by construction.
  3. **Totally change direction**: after 4 s still blocked, a full A* re-plan from the current position through the waypoint graph (a genuinely different route). After that: stand and chat until the obstacle moves or the period changes. Counters reset on any real progress.
  4. **Distance bump**: MIN_SEPARATION 0.7 -> 0.8 m and arrival rings 0.8/1.2/1.6 m - the v1 0.7 m clusters read as "hugging" (Lucas: "they just stand in the group very very close, like hugging ;P").
  5. The right-hand rule survives as the detour SIDE preference (right first, left as fallback), not as continuous steering.
- **Amended 2026-09-01 (v2 playtest -> v3, Lucas: "they now just stay and talk from natural distance, so it is better than it was, but they never stops! They just first try to move, change position, looks ok, but if the group is big some people in the middle will just stop trying to get out. maybe we just need to loop, at least loop on last element, so the movement in new direction, even opposite direction if needed, but ideally just to avoid colision."):** v2's blocked ladder was TERMINAL and could not escalate, so a jammed NPC gave up permanently. Three defects, all in the ladder: (1) `if (insertDetour(...)) state.detourCount = 1` - a detour that FAILED (both candidate points occupied, which is exactly the case for someone in the middle of a group) left `detourCount` at 0, so the chain re-entered the same first branch every frame and never reached the left detour or the re-plan; (2) the A* re-plan was guarded by `!replannedForBlock`, so it fired once per block episode, ever - after that the ladder had no rungs left; (3) only two candidate directions existed (0.9 m right, 0.9 m left), and in a crowd both are occupied, so there was nothing valid to pick. v3 makes the ladder a LOOP that never gives up:
  1. **Escape fan, not two fixed sides** (`escapeWaypoint`): candidate directions widen from the walk heading - 60 deg right, 60 deg left, 90 deg, 120 deg, then STRAIGHT BACK (the "even opposite direction if needed") - at 1.0 m and 1.5 m, and the fan is ROTATED by the attempt index so consecutive retries lead with a different direction (this also breaks the mirror symmetry between two NPCs facing off). A candidate is valid when it is off furniture, has nobody within MIN_SEPARATION, and the straight line to it is clear.
  2. **Last-resort radial squeeze-out:** if every fan candidate fails (the dense-crowd middle), the final candidate steps 1.0 m directly AWAY from the local crowd centroid with the capsule requirement dropped - hard separation still guarantees nobody is overlapped, so the NPC always has somewhere to go.
  3. **The ladder loops forever:** a blocked NPC retries every `ESCAPE_RETRY_S` (1.5 s) after a 1.2 s "talk a moment" grace, incrementing an attempt counter with no terminal state; every 3rd attempt is a full A* re-plan instead of a local escape. Nothing is once-only, and a failed attempt no longer swallows the escalation - the counter advances regardless of success.
  4. **The escape leg is protected:** while an escape waypoint is pending (until the NPC actually passes it) the stop-at-distance capsule check is suspended for that NPC, so the escape move cannot be frozen by the very rule that triggered it.
  5. **A head-on pair breaks symmetry** (`givesWayTo`, `blockerAhead`): two NPCs blocking each other ran the identical rule on the identical timer, so both escaped at the same instant, both came back, and they paced in sync forever (Lucas: "they loop over and over by getting back few steps, stopping, and getting back again to the exact same place they come from ... both do the same in the loop, almost in sync"). The controller now tracks WHO is in each walker's lane; when the block is mutual, an antisymmetric id comparison picks exactly one to step aside while the other holds the lane and keeps chatting (capped at 2 waits so a stalled partner cannot freeze it). Measured on a flanked lane where sidestepping is impossible: direction reversals went from 18 for the pair to 4, and both arrive.
  6. **The ladder REMEMBERS** (`ESCAPE_MEMORY_S` = 3 s): the escape attempt counter used to reset on ANY progress, so a pair that bumped, backed off and re-approached restarted at rung one every cycle - a limit cycle by construction. It now survives short bursts of movement and only clears after sustained clear walking, so repeated collisions with the same NPC escalate through the fan and into a re-plan.
  7. **The crowd PARTS - the actual root cause.** Even with the loop, a surrounded NPC still could not move, and the reason was the separation constraint itself: passing between two NPCs needs a gap of 2 x MIN_SEPARATION (1.6 m), and a real cluster's gaps are 0.8-1.5 m, so hard separation builds an impassable FENCE around whoever ends up in the middle - the escape waypoints were being found and were valid, the walker simply could never reach them. Fixed by making separation yield to movement intent: while a walker is actively escaping (`escapeIndex >= 0` or blocked past the grace period), the pair floor between it and its blockers drops to `SQUEEZE_SEPARATION` (0.62 m - NPC bodies are 0.3 m in radius, so 0.6 m is exactly touching: a brush-past, never an overlap), and settled blockers take HALF the correction instead of being immovable, so the cluster physically opens. Each settled NPC remembers the spot where it settled (`anchor`) and walks back to it at 0.6 m/s once that spot is free again, so the crowd closes back up and nobody drifts off their desk. Verified on the reproduction: an NPC ringed by six settled NPCs at 0.85 m (every gap 0.86 m) went from NEVER escaping (0.06 m of movement in 120 s) to reaching its exact target in 6.5 s, with the closest approach at any moment 0.62 m and every ring member back on its original spot afterwards.

### C-50 — NPC crowd flow: measured, not guessed (2026-09-01)

- **Source (Lucas, 2026-09-01):** "no, it is not better at all! mabe even worse, more of people get stack this time.... same issue, go back and forth in a loop." Plus two proposals: "they just should avoid going back to the same place ... npc need to rotate in different angle. Or just always take random number to choose the rotation" and "NPC could always use random sequence of moves, not in the same order (higher chance to go in oposite direction, not in sync)."
- **Method change (the important part):** C-48 v1-v3 were tuned against hand-built scenarios that all passed while the real game kept failing. This round starts from a MEASUREMENT instead: `tests/unit/npc-office-day.test.ts` simulates a full 1200 s day with all 14 NPCs on their real schedules (morning entry crowd, lunch rush, evening) and records, per NPC, time frozen while in the walking state, longest continuous freeze, jam episodes over 2 s, direction reversals, and distance walked. The committed C-48 v3 controller scored **2014 s frozen and 375 jam episodes** on that scenario - the bug, quantified. Every change below was kept or discarded on its measured effect, and the harness is now a regression test with ceilings.
- **What was actually wrong (four causes, none of them the escape ladder):**
  1. **The stop distance was larger than the separation distance** (1.1 m vs 0.8 m), so an NPC halted before it was ever close enough for the separation constraint to engage - the mechanism that slides two people past each other could never fire, and every encounter became a standstill plus a negotiation. `BLOCKED_LOOKAHEAD` is now 0.75 m, under the floor, with a unit test pinning the relationship.
  2. **The path planner did not know NPCs exist.** `planNpcPath` only ever saw furniture, so "re-plan a different route" returned the identical straight line and the NPC walked back into the same person. Standing NPCs are now fed to the planner as temporary AABBs (`blockerBoxes`), so a re-route genuinely avoids them; routes over `ROUTE_DETOUR_LIMIT` are rejected, and a `REPLAN_COOLDOWN_S` commitment stops the NPC flip-flopping between two alternatives (that alone cut one NPC from 905 m walked in a day to 71 m).
  3. **LIVELOCK was invisible.** Path advance drove an NPC forward while hard separation shoved it back, so it paced the same two metres forever - and because it moved every single frame it never registered as "blocked", so no stuck-policy ever fired. Net displacement over a 4 s window is now the test of progress; failing it arms the whole blocked policy including the squeeze floor. Measured on the reported case: an NPC oscillating in a 2.3 m stretch for 250 s+.
  4. **Trying forever beats nothing.** A trip now gets a budget (planned duration x3 plus 12 s grace); past it the NPC settles where it stands and chats until the next period re-plans everyone. Standing somewhere slightly unintended is strictly better than visibly struggling for minutes - Lucas's own preference, stated twice.
- **Lucas's two proposals, both implemented:** the escape fan is SHUFFLED per NPC from the controller's rng (`EscapeOptions.rng`), so two NPCs cannot pick mirrored moves in lockstep, and the retry cadence is jittered per NPC for the same reason; and a spot the NPC just retreated from is off-limits to later escape candidates (`RETURN_BLOCK_RADIUS`, one spot, 3 s). On top of those, an escape that leaves the NPC no closer to its destination counts as futile, and two futile escapes send it into a calm wait instead of another shuffle.
- **Tried and REJECTED on measurement:** creeping forward at 35% speed instead of stopping when someone is in the lane. It reads well in theory (crowds flow, they do not halt) but pressing forward fights the separation constraint every frame and spreads the contention - jam episodes went 63 -> 155 and total freezing 261 s -> 586 s. Reverted; the comment in `npc-controller.ts` records the numbers so it is not retried blind.
- **Result on the same measured day:** frozen-while-walking **2014 s -> 265 s**, jam episodes **375 -> 53**, worst single NPC distance walked **905 m -> 115 m**, longest freeze under 7.5 s. Ceilings are asserted in the regression test with headroom.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (1) No NPC paces back and forth for minutes. (2) No NPC stands frozen mid-walk for more than a few seconds. (3) A walk that cannot be completed ends in the NPC settling and chatting, never in an endless loop. (4) The day simulation stays under its ceilings.


### C-51 — Morning arrivals: a staggered office, not a factory gate (2026-09-01)

- **Source (Lucas, 2026-09-01):** "AFAIK in PRD we had that some people may be late to the office, and all people should have some random delay so they do not apear all at once in the morning. Some may even be already on their desks if they get earlier than the player, to make it more natural for IT company. It's not a manufactory where they open the gate and all workers get inside at once. can you check and fix that? Fix how our morning schedule works right now. We will avoid also coilisions in this way, in crawd some npc struggle to get out of it."
- **Was:** `npc-controller.ts` had exactly one arrival mechanism, gated on `lastPeriod === null` so it ran once per controller lifetime. It teleported all 13 humans onto the SINGLE door point (0, 0, 8.4) on frame 0, left them standing there visibly stacked, and released them within `baseDelay * 8 + jitter` = 0..9.5 s - onto 13 different outbound paths from the identical spot. Four defects: (1) no spread in space and 9.5 s of spread in time on a 180 s period; (2) nobody was already at a desk, contradicting PRD 11.4's fade-in description ("some already at their desks (Marek, Bartek)"); (3) nobody was late, so PRD 11.1's "Janusz arrives late (10am)" was never implemented; (4) the day-1-only gate meant that on day 2+ `synchronizePeriod("morning")` re-planned everyone from (0, 0, 0) - where `gone-home` had parked them - and the whole company popped into existence in the middle of the office.
- **The measurement that drove this (C-50 method, morning only, 14 NPCs on real schedules, seed 2024):** peak NPCs within 3 m of the door **13 of 14**; frozen-while-walking across the morning **165.5 s**, of which **147.2 s (89%) happened at the door**; Marek's ~16 m door-to-desk walk (~11 s at his 1.4 m/s) took **67.9 s**; worst individual freezes Janusz 30.8 s, Marek 28.0 s, Tomek 27.1 s, Klaudia 24.5 s. So the crowd-jam Lucas reported was not a separate avoidance bug - the morning gate WAS the crowd, and 89% of the morning's jamming is created by it. This is why the fix belongs in the arrival data and not in another round of avoidance tuning.
- **Now (arrival profile as pure data in `npc-schedule.ts`, executed by the controller):**
  1. **Two modes.** `already-in` places the NPC directly on their morning schedule entry at day start (no door, no walk). `arrives` keeps the NPC invisible and parked off-scene until its moment, then places it at the door and gives it a normal path.
  2. **Roster (Lucas's choice, 2026-09-01):** already in = **Bartek, Marek** (both named in PRD 11.4), **Maciek** the CTO (11.1: "only appears in the morning"), **Dawid** the CEO (his own office), **Burek** the dog. Arriving over the first ~95 s of the 180 s morning = Zosia, Tomek, Kasia, Pawel, Grazyna, Ania, Klaudia, Przemek. Late at ~130 s = **Janusz**, implementing 11.1's "arrives late (10am)".
  3. **Minimum inter-arrival gap is the real fix.** Offsets are sorted then pushed so consecutive arrivals are >= `MIN_ARRIVAL_GAP_S` (4 s) apart - ~4.8 m of clearance at 1.2 m/s, so each arrival is out of the doorway before the next appears. The crowd is prevented in data rather than untangled at runtime by C-48/C-50.
  4. **At most one body in the doorway,** because a not-yet-arrived NPC is invisible and off-scene rather than stacked on the door point.
  5. **Stable personality, varied day:** id-hash base offset (Bartek is always early, Janusz always last) + per-day seeded rng jitter + a lateral door offset in [-0.8, 0.8] so consecutive arrivals do not retrace one point.
  6. **Runs every day,** keyed on the day counter instead of controller construction - no more day-2 pop-in at the office centre.
- **Found while implementing - the corridor graph had no door node.** With arrivals staggered the door crowd was gone, but the measured door-freeze only fell 147.2 s -> 41.1 s, and a trace showed why: `nearestReachableWaypoint` snaps a walker to the closest waypoint by raw distance, and from the door (0, 8.4) that is `door-main-meeting` (0, 9.9) at 1.5 m - BEHIND the arriving NPC, inside the meeting room - not `main-south` (0, 5) at 3.4 m ahead. Every arrival routed north into the meeting room and back; Klaudia paced that loop for 23.7 s. Added `door-main-entry` at (0, 8.4) to `corridor-waypoints.ts`. This is a general path-planning fix that happened to be exposed by arrivals - anything starting a walk from the office door was affected.
- **Waiting NPCs are inert.** They are parked INVISIBLE on their own doormat rather than off-scene: every consumer of NPC positions already filters on `visible` (movement snapshot, separation, chatter pairing, anchor return), and releasing them then costs zero movement, which keeps the crowd-flow metrics honest (parking them off-scene showed up as a 1414 m "walk" in the day harness). Two consumers did not filter and now do: the roster shows **"Not in yet"** on a disabled card (C-46 truthful-location rule), and `pickFromCamera` filters invisible meshes - three.js does NOT skip invisible objects when raycasting, so the player could otherwise have hovered and clicked a body that was not in the room.
- **Overrides respect the door.** `rollRandomNpcDestinations` skips NPCs who have not arrived (new `NpcController.hasArrived`), so the day's destination roll cannot pull someone in early - they head for their desk when they walk in. A deliberate `setOverride` on a waiting NPC releases them through the door first instead of teleporting them mid-office. `createNpcController` also takes an `{ arrivals }` option (default true) that the C-48/C-50 physics tests set false, since they place NPCs directly.
- **Superseded test:** C-48's "spreads the morning door crowd instead of stacking on one point" pinned the factory-gate behaviour (everyone on (0, 8.4), spread by the separation pass). C-51 removes the stack at the source, so it is replaced by the opposite contract - with arrivals on, at most one NPC is ever on the door point.
- **Measured result.** Morning: peak NPCs within 3 m of the door **13 of 14 -> 1**; frozen-at-the-door **147.2 s -> 15.7 s**; frozen across the whole morning **165.5 s -> 31.0 s**. Full day on the C-50 harness: frozen **265 s -> 77 s**, jam episodes **53 -> 19**, worst single walk **115 m -> 59 m**. The day-regression ceilings are tightened to 250 s / 60 episodes / 150 m / 12 s. So C-51 also turned out to be the largest remaining win on the crowd problem C-48 and C-50 were chasing - most of what those rounds had left to fix was being manufactured by the morning gate.
- **Janusz note:** only his arrival TIME changes. His schedule state stays `at-desk` in all three periods, so L-2026-08-31-02 and its two unit tests are unaffected.
- **Acceptance criteria:** (1) At day start several colleagues are already working and several walk in through the door. (2) Only a handful of NPCs are ever near the door at once. (3) Janusz arrives visibly later than everyone else. (4) Day 2+ mornings arrive through the door exactly like day 1. (5) The morning door-jam metrics stay under the ceilings in `tests/unit/npc-morning-arrivals.test.ts`.


### C-52 — "End the day" actually ends the day (2026-09-01)

### C-54 — Conversations are staged: stand next to the NPC, keep eye contact, stay after Skip (2026-09-01)

- **Was:** opening a dialogue panned the camera (`cameraDirector.panTo`, offset (0, 1.6, 3.2) from the NPC) with no collision awareness, so the view landed inside walls/furniture; on close the camera snapped back to the player's feet - the spawn point when talking from the roster - i.e. "reset to the starting position".
