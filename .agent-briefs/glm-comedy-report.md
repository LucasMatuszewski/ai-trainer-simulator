BRAINDUMP: naming + tone design for the 3D retro pixel-art trainer-survival game

=====================================================================
1. GAME TITLES (5 options)
=====================================================================

1. LOCALHOST LEGEND
   You are a legend. On localhost. Nowhere else. (Bonus: the
   game's final rank ladder can quietly grow this joke: Legend
   of Localhost, Hero of Production, Guardian of the Region,
   Champion of the GALAXY.)

2. SLIDES OF WRATH
   Death by slides, Grapes of Wrath, same suffering, different
   dust bowl. Screams "trainer", reads as pure misery.

3. TRAINER AS A SERVICE
   You have subscription-boxed yourself. Enterprise IT eats
   services; today it is eating you.

4. YOU ARE NOW UNMUTED
   The four most terrifying words in modern training. The title
   IS the jump scare.

5. CONSULTANT OF THE GALAXY
   The endgame goal said out loud with a straight face. Broke
   today, cosmic tomorrow, invoice still unpaid either way.

=====================================================================
2. NPC CHARACTER CONCEPTS (5)
=====================================================================

MIREK, "The 503" - Principal Engineer at your biggest client.
The 10x engineer who ships more correct code before 9am than the
whole team ships in a sprint, and who cannot order a sandwich
without filing a ticket first. Hood up, headphones on, speaks
exclusively in HTTP status codes and commit messages. Warming to
you exactly one degree per fiscal year, ever since you drew a
load balancer on the whiteboard correctly without saying "cloud".
He is silently keeping the entire company alive and the company
does not know he exists.
Signature line: "Small talk detected. Returning 503."

BOGDAN, "The Evangelist" - Client-side "Digital Transformation
Evangelist" whose LinkedIn headline is longer than his actual CV.
Posts carousels about resilience, photographs his flat white like
it is evidence, and is contractually unable to say anything
unless it starts with "humbled" or "thrilled to announce". He
will promote your courses for free, loudly, and catastrophically
wrongly. Genuinely believes he is your friend, which is somehow
the saddest part.
Signature line: "I don't sell courses. I build movements. The
movement also sells courses."

AGNIESZKA, "Six Minutes" - Freelance enterprise consultant, 25
years in, has survived four recessions and one SAP rollout
(she does not talk about the rollout). Bills in 6-minute
increments, including for the time spent calculating the
increments. Your unwilling mentor in the dark art of not dying
of poverty: warm in the exact way an invoice is warm. Everything
she knows, you need. Everything she says, costs.
Signature line: "Advice is free for the first six minutes.
You're at four. Choose your next words carefully."

KEVIN, "The Believer" - Junior developer at the client company.
Believes npm install is magic, Stack Overflow is scripture, and
the terminal is a personality. His questions to you (the
trainer) are 40% startling genius and 60% potential war crimes.
He is simultaneously the reason you got into teaching and the
reason you started drinking. Would fax his heart to you if you
asked.
Signature line: "It said 'found 0 vulnerabilities'. That means
the code is perfect, right?"

GREG, "The Orbit" - Client Engagement Manager. Has never once
finished a sentence. Parks, circles back, aligns, actions,
socializes, and takes things offline, including things that are
already offline and were said face to face. Nobody in company
history has extracted a "yes" or a "no" from him. His calendar
is completely full and completely empty. Controls whether you
get rebooked, which makes him the most dangerous man alive.
Signature line: "Let's park that, circle back, and take it
offline. So: no."

4. MINI-GAME CONCEPTS (5)
=====================================================================

THE DONGLE CRISIS
Ninety seconds before the workshop starts, the projector rejects
your laptop and accepts exactly one connector standard, which
exists somewhere in the client's Drawer Of A Thousand Cables.
The drawer contains every standard since 1987, including one
that may predate electricity. Somewhere in the building, forty
participants finish their coffee and start checking email. Dig.
Comedy engine: pure prop-shop time pressure with a very real
stake (your entire livelihood is an HDMI away from collapse).

TOAST ARCHITECTURE
A CEO with the attention span of a goldfish asks what a neural
network is. You have thirty seconds and, for legal reasons, only
breakfast metaphors. The room only truly nods once the GPU is a
toaster, the data is butter, and "we are selling toast at
scale". Someone writes the word "toast" into next year's budget.
You have won. You have also lost. Historians will disagree.

THE Q&A GAUNTLET
Your talk just ended and hands go up. Triage at speed: the man
whose "question" is a four-minute pitch for his own startup, the
woman asking something you genuinely do not know, and the guy in
the back asking "is this in the exam?". You have exactly three
moves - honesty, slide 47, and "great question, let's take that
offline" - and every one of them costs you something different.

NET-90: THE CALL
Chase an invoice that is older than some of your students. Crawl
through the hold menu ("press 1 to be ignored in Polish, press 2
to be ignored in English"), endure hold music that is your own
course jingle slowed down 400%, and stay within the boundaries
of professional courtesy while the game whispers, in real time,
what you could say instead. Rent is due Friday.

PRINTER DUEL
Handouts are needed in three minutes. The office printer has
jammed in a manner that violates several laws of physics, and it
has done so with intent. This is the IT Crowd homage fight scene:
you versus the machine, thirty seconds, no witnesses. Your
special move is turning it off and on again. Your finisher is
reading the manual. Nobody has ever seen the finisher.

=====================================================================
5. EASTER EGGS / HIDDEN GEMS (5)
=====================================================================

THE UNTOUCHABLE SERVER
In the server room, one ancient tower still humming under a
small memorial plaque: "SRV-APP-03. 2014-present. Nobody knows
what it does. Nobody touches it. It has outlived three CTOs."
On the monitor next to it, a sticky note reads "login:
admin / Password123!" and beneath it, in different handwriting,
"changed: never".

THE DUCK SHELF
One desk holds 47 rubber ducks, each with a tiny name tag of the
bug it "solved" (OffByOne, TimeZoneDemon, TheFridayBug). The
48th duck is turned to face the wall. Its tag reads: "He has
seen things."

THE CORNER
In a dusty storage room, an old CRT runs a DVD-logo screensaver
bounce, endlessly. Stand there long enough and it finally hits
the corner perfectly - somewhere deep in the building, something
chimes, once. The player is rewarded with absolutely nothing
else, which is the joke, and players will still do it.

LOCALHOST / 127.0.0.1
Two meeting room doors on opposite sides of the floor, labeled
"localhost" and "127.0.0.1". They are the same room. No NPC ever
comments on this. Booking either one books both.

THE AI STRATEGY
On the client's boardroom table, a printed Word document:
"AI_STRATEGY_FINAL_v9_USE_THIS_ONE.docx". It contains two lines.
Line one: "TODO". Line two: "ask the trainer when he arrives".
Their entire AI strategy is you, and they printed it, and it is
somehow still the most honest document in the building.
